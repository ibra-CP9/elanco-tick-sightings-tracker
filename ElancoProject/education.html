Species Identification Guide

Explore the five tick species included in the Tick Sightings Tracker dataset.

Southern Rodent Tick

Ixodes acuminatus

Typical hosts: Small rodents.

Usually inhabits rodent burrows and nests. UK records are mainly from southern England.

Passerine Tick

Ixodes frontalis

Typical hosts: Wild birds.

Commonly associated with bird nests and woodland, parks and gardens. It can occasionally feed on humans.

Fox/Badger Tick

Ixodes canisuga

Typical hosts: Foxes, badgers and dogs.

Often associated with the burrows and resting places of its mammal hosts.

Marsh Tick

Ixodes apronophorus

Typical habitat: Wetlands.

Associated with water voles and wetland environments.

Tree-hole Tick

Ixodes arboricola

Typical hosts: Birds nesting in tree holes.

Often found in tree-hole nests used by birds such as blue tits and great tits.

Species information: UK Health Security Agency tick species profiles .

Tick Bite Prevention

Follow these precautions when walking, gardening or spending time outdoors.

Wear suitable clothing

Wear long sleeves and trousers. Tuck trousers into socks when walking through vegetation.

Use insect repellent

Consider a suitable tick repellent and follow the instructions on its label.

Check your body

After being outdoors, check your skin, clothing and body for ticks. Check children and pets too.

Remove ticks promptly

If a tick is attached, remove it promptly with a tick-removal tool or fine-tipped tweezers, following NHS guidance.

For further advice, visit the NHS Lyme disease guidance .
